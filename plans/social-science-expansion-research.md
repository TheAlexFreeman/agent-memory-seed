# Research Plan: Social Science Knowledge Base Expansion

## Goals

Expand the social science knowledge base beyond cultural evolution into adjacent domains that:

1. **Extend the epistemic story** — Cultural evolution explains *how* ideas propagate (transmission biases, prestige, fitness vs truth). Sociology of knowledge and STS explain *how* scientific knowledge is produced, contested, and stabilized — the institutional machinery behind idea fitness.
2. **Ground collective behavior in game theory** — Game theory (already present) provides the formal structure; collective action theory (Olson, Ostrom, North) provides the social-institutional application. How do groups solve coordination and cooperation problems?
3. **Add empirical social psychology** — Conformity (Asch), obedience (Milgram), and group dynamics provide the experimental bedrock for transmission biases. Cultural evolution invokes "conformist bias" and "prestige bias" — social psychology operationalizes and measures them.
4. **Bridge to behavioral economics** — Kahneman/Tversky's heuristics and biases connect cognitive science (attention, metacognition) to economic choice. Rationality, calibration, and decision-making under uncertainty are central to Alex's interests.
5. **Introduce network structure** — Cultural evolution treats ideas and populations; network science adds *structure* — who is connected to whom, how information diffuses through networks, wisdom of crowds vs groupthink. Connects to `mathematics/dynamical-systems/complex-networks-small-world-scale-free.md`.
6. **Deepen social epistemology** — Fricker (epistemic injustice) is present; Goldman and extended epistemology broaden the framework for how communities produce and distribute knowledge.

## Phases

### Phase 1 — Sociology of Knowledge & Science and Technology Studies

*Addresses Gap A. Highest priority: completes the epistemic story begun in cultural evolution.*

Target subfolder: `knowledge/_unverified/social-science/sociology-of-knowledge/`

**Files to research and write:**

1. **`mannheim-sociology-of-knowledge.md`** — Karl Mannheim's *Ideology and Utopia* (1929): knowledge as socially situated; the sociology of knowledge as the study of how social position shapes thought; ideology and utopia as functional belief systems. The "problem of the free-floating intellectual." Limits and critiques.

2. **`merton-scientific-norms.md`** — Robert K. Merton's CUDOS norms (Communalism, Universalism, Disinterestedness, Organized Skepticism): the institutional structure that makes science a truth-tracking social system. Priority and the Matthew effect. The normative vs descriptive debate — do scientists actually follow these norms?

3. **`kuhn-paradigms-scientific-revolutions.md`** — Thomas Kuhn's *The Structure of Scientific Revolutions* (1962): normal science, paradigms, puzzles, anomalies; crises and revolutions; incommensurability. Why paradigm shifts are not purely rational. Connection to AI paradigm genealogy and the cultural evolution of technical ideas.

4. **`latour-actor-network-theory.md`** — Bruno Latour and actor-network theory (ANT): science as a network of human and nonhuman actors; the construction of facts through trials of strength; "follow the actors." Symmetry principle. Relevance to AI systems as actors in sociotechnical networks.

5. **`social-construction-of-scientific-knowledge.md`** — Synthesis: the strong programme (Bloor), the science wars, the difference between "knowledge is socially produced" (uncontroversial) and "knowledge is merely social" (controversial). How this connects to idea fitness vs truth and epistemic virtue in communities.

### Phase 2 — Collective Action & Institutional Economics

*Addresses Gap B. Connects game theory to real-world coordination and governance.*

Target subfolder: `knowledge/_unverified/social-science/collective-action/`

**Files to research and write:**

1. **`olson-logic-of-collective-action.md`** — Mancur Olson's *The Logic of Collective Action* (1965): the free-rider problem; why rational self-interested individuals will not voluntarily contribute to collective goods; large vs small groups; selective incentives. The foundational paradox of collective action.

2. **`ostrom-governing-the-commons.md`** — Elinor Ostrom's *Governing the Commons* (1990): polycentric governance; the eight design principles for successful common-pool resource management; evidence that communities can self-organize without state or market. Critique of Hardin's tragedy of the commons. Relevance to open-source, AI governance, coordination failures.

3. **`north-institutions-institutional-change.md`** — Douglass North's institutional economics: institutions as rules of the game; formal vs informal institutions; transaction costs; path dependence; institutional change as a source of economic growth and stagnation. Connects to cultural evolution (norms as institutions).

4. **`acemoglu-robinson-inclusive-institutions.md`** — Acemoglu & Robinson's *Why Nations Fail*: extractive vs inclusive institutions; institutional drift and critical junctures; the role of political and economic institutions in long-run development. Simplifications and critiques.

5. **`collective-action-synthesis-ai-governance.md`** — Synthesis: when does collective action succeed or fail? Ostrom's principles vs Olson's pessimism. Application to AI safety coordination, multi-stakeholder governance, and the tragedy of the commons in AI capability race dynamics.

### Phase 3 — Social Psychology: Conformity, Obedience & Group Dynamics

*Addresses Gap C. Empirical grounding for cultural evolution's transmission biases.*

Target subfolder: `knowledge/_unverified/social-science/social-psychology/`

**Files to research and write:**

1. **`asch-conformity-experiments.md`** — Solomon Asch's line-length experiments (1950s): majority influence, normative vs informational conformity; the power of unanimity; individual differences. Direct empirical parallel to Boyd & Richerson's conformist bias.

2. **`milgram-obedience-experiments.md`** — Stanley Milgram's obedience studies (1961–62): the agentic state; situational determinants of obedience; ethical controversies and replications. What this reveals about hierarchical transmission and authority bias.

3. **`zimbardo-stanford-prison-situation.md`** — Philip Zimbardo's Stanford prison experiment: situational power, role internalization, deindividuation. Critiques (demand characteristics, lack of replicability). The broader lesson about how situations shape behavior.

4. **`group-polarization-groupthink.md`** — Group polarization (Moscovici, Sunstein): groups move toward extremity. Irving Janis's groupthink: premature consensus, suppression of dissent. When groups improve vs degrade judgment.

5. **`bystander-effect-diffusion-responsibility.md`** — Latané and Darley's bystander effect: diffusion of responsibility; pluralistic ignorance; the unresponsive bystander. Relevance to collective inaction and coordination failures.

6. **`social-psychology-transmission-biases-synthesis.md`** — Synthesis: mapping Asch → conformist bias, Milgram → prestige/authority bias, group polarization → echo chambers. How social psychology predicts and explains cultural transmission dynamics.

### Phase 4 — Behavioral Economics: Heuristics, Biases & Nudges

*Addresses Gap D. Connects cognitive science to economic choice and rationality.*

Target subfolder: `knowledge/_unverified/social-science/behavioral-economics/`

**Files to research and write:**

1. **`kahneman-tversky-heuristics-biases.md`** — Amos Tversky and Daniel Kahneman's heuristics program: availability, representativeness, anchoring and adjustment. Systematic biases these produce. The "heuristics and biases" vs "fast and slow" framing. Connection to cognitive science (attention, availability-representativeness-anchoring).

2. **`prospect-theory-loss-aversion.md`** — Prospect theory (Kahneman & Tversky, 1979): reference dependence, loss aversion, probability weighting. Why expected utility fails descriptively. The endowment effect, status quo bias.

4. **`bounded-rationality-simon.md`** — Herbert Simon's bounded rationality: satisficing, procedural rationality, the limits of optimization. Contrast with game-theoretic rationality. Relevance to AI alignment (what kind of rationality do we want to align to?).

5. **`behavioral-economics-rationality-synthesis.md`** — Synthesis: when do biases matter? Domain specificity, individual differences, debiasing. The rationality debate in light of heuristics and biases. Connection to rationalist community epistemology and calibration.

### Phase 5 — Network Science & Social Diffusion

*Addresses Gap E. Structure of social transmission; complements cultural evolution.*

Target subfolder: `knowledge/_unverified/social-science/network-diffusion/`

**Files to research and write:**

1. **`granovetter-weak-ties-strength.md`** — Mark Granovetter's "The Strength of Weak Ties" (1973): weak ties bridge clusters; strong ties create cohesion; job information flows through weak ties. Structural holes (Burt). Relevance to information diversity and echo chambers.

2. **`rogers-diffusion-of-innovations.md`** — Everett Rogers' *Diffusion of Innovations* (1962): innovators, early adopters, early majority, late majority, laggards; the S-curve of adoption; attributes that affect diffusion (relative advantage, compatibility, complexity, trialability, observability). Connects to prestige-cascades-llm-adoption.

3. **`watts-information-cascades.md`** — Duncan Watts on information cascades: when do individuals follow the crowd? Sequential decision models; the fragility of cascades; the role of network structure. Connection to conformity and herding.

4. **`surowiecki-wisdom-of-crowds.md`** — James Surowiecki's *The Wisdom of Crowds*: when aggregating diverse independent judgments beats experts; the conditions (diversity, independence, decentralization, aggregation); when crowds fail (groupthink, herding). Relevance to prediction markets, meta-forecasting, AI alignment (collective intelligence vs collective stupidity).

5. **`network-diffusion-synthesis.md`** — Synthesis: network structure + transmission biases = diffusion dynamics. Small-world and scale-free properties (from mathematics) applied to social and informational contagion. How LLMs change the topology of cultural transmission.
