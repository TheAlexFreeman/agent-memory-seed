# Research Plan: LessWrong and the Rationalist Community

## Goals

Alex wants a clear narrative account of the LessWrong / Rationalist community: where it came from, what problems it thought it was solving, how it drew on existing academic literatures and internet subcultures, and how a broader community formed around figures and institutions such as Eliezer Yudkowsky, Robin Hanson, Scott Alexander, Gwern, MIRI, and CFAR.

This plan is not just a reading list on individual personalities. The aim is to reconstruct the community as an intellectual and social formation: a blend of heuristics-and-biases psychology, Bayesian and decision-theoretic thinking, futurism / transhumanism, internet blog culture, meetup and workshop practices, and later AI-risk / effective-altruist overlap.

Across all files, keep four through-lines in view:

1. **Intellectual sources** — heuristics and biases, bounded rationality, Bayesianism, decision theory, prediction, economics, and adjacent cognitive-science traditions.
2. **Founders and interpreters** — Yudkowsky and the Sequences first, then Hanson, Scott Alexander, Gwern, and other key translators or adjacent figures.
3. **Institutions and practices** — LessWrong itself, MIRI, CFAR, meetups, workshops, HPMOR, forecasting, and the attempt to turn "rationality" into a lived discipline.
4. **Aims and tensions** — epistemic and instrumental rationality, self-improvement, existential risk, AI alignment, civilizational competence, and the recurrent criticism that the community overreaches the science it cites.

## Research phases and priority order

### Phase 1 — Yudkowsky and the Sequences (highest priority) · ☑ 2/2 complete

The community makes the least sense if Yudkowsky is treated as just "one blogger among others." Start by reconstructing his role as founder-theorist and the Sequences as the canon-forming text.

1. ☑ `origins/eliezer-yudkowsky-intellectual-biography.md`
   - Early internet background: singularitarianism, transhumanist / Extropian influence, and the founding context of the Singularity Institute
   - Yudkowsky's central obsessions: AGI, recursive self-improvement, existential risk, reductionism, metaethics, and "raising the sanity waterline"
   - His role as writer-founder rather than conventional academic, and how that shaped the movement's self-conception
   - Distinguish biography, doctrine, and institutional entrepreneurship instead of collapsing them together

2. ☑ `origins/the-sequences-core-arguments.md`
   - Map the major arcs of the Sequences: map/territory, beliefs as anticipations, reductionism, Bayes, heuristics and biases, mysterious answers, politics as mind-killer, tabooing words, metaethics, and instrumental rationality
   - Ask which arguments are direct borrowings, which are syntheses, and which are distinctively Yudkowskian
   - Explain why the Sequences functioned as pedagogy and initiation text, not just a set of essays

### Phase 2 — Source literatures and prehistory (high priority) · ☑ 2/2 complete

Before LessWrong became a community, there were already academic and online traditions supplying concepts, norms, and rhetorical habits.

3. ☑ `origins/heuristics-biases-bayes-and-bounded-rationality.md`
   - Kahneman and Tversky, Herbert Simon, Gigerenzer, Bayesianism, decision theory, and forecasting traditions
   - How LessWrong and CFAR-style rationality reinterpret these literatures as practical debiasing and self-cultivation
   - Where the community tracks the science closely and where it turns contested research into stronger normative claims than the literature supports

4. ☑ `origins/academic-and-online-prehistory.md`
   - Extropians, cryonics, transhumanism, early AI futurism, internet mailing lists / Usenet / blogs, economics and prediction-market culture, and secular-libertarian-science-fiction milieus
   - The 2000s blogosphere as the medium that made a text-heavy, argument-intensive subculture possible
   - Separate genuine lineages from retrospective mythmaking

This phase explains how an intellectual tendency became a recognizable social world.

5. ☑ `origins/robin-hanson-overcoming-bias-and-the-founding-split.md`

   - How jargon, argument style, and social technologies created a sense of shared identity

### Phase 4 — Major later and adjacent writers (high priority) · ☑ 2/2 complete

The community's self-understanding did not remain purely Yudkowskian. Scott Alexander and Gwern both helped broaden what "rationalist" writing could look like.

7. ☑ `figures/scott-alexander-and-the-broadening-of-rationalism.md`
   - Slate Star Codex / Astral Codex Ten as a translation layer from core rationalism into a wider essay-reading public
   - Scott's tone, psychiatry background, empiricist caution, and tendency to humanize or soften harder-edged movement rhetoric
   - Where Scott aligns with Yudkowsky-style rationalism and where he diverges

8. ☑ `figures/gwern-and-the-rationalist-research-ethos.md`
   - Gwern as adjacent but influential: long-form internet scholarship, exhaustive citation, self-experimentation, statistical curiosity, and archiving
   - His role in shaping a style of "amateur but serious" inquiry that overlaps strongly with rationalist norms without being reducible to institution-centered LessWrong culture
   - Distinguish influence on method and tone from formal community centrality

   - The division of labor among LessWrong, MIRI, CFAR, Alignment Forum, and later effective-altruist institutions

10. ☑ `community/meetups-workshops-hpmor-and-lived-rationality.md`
   - Meetups, group houses, workshops, forecasting, self-experimentation, and attempts to make rationality into everyday practice
   - HPMOR as recruitment, identity text, and bridge between fiction fandom and rationalist pedagogy
   - Which community practices are central, which are peripheral, and which are mostly outsider stereotypes

## Research approach and practical notes

- Each file: ~2500-4000 words. Prefer narrative explanation over encyclopedic coverage. The point is to understand the community's development, not to catalog every post or controversy.
- Start with primary sources whenever possible: the Sequences, Overcoming Bias, early LessWrong posts, MIRI and CFAR materials, Slate Star Codex / Astral Codex Ten, and Gwern's own essays. Use secondary sources to contextualize rather than replace primary reading.
- Distinguish three layers in every file: what participants said about themselves, what the underlying literature actually supports, and what outside observers claimed about the movement.
- Do not treat "the rationalist community" as a monolith. Track at least four overlapping but non-identical populations: early LessWrong core, SSC / ACX-adjacent readers, AI-alignment / MIRI circles, and the CFAR / meetup / applied-rationality world.
- Be explicit about disagreements and drift. Hanson is not Yudkowsky; Scott Alexander is not CFAR; Gwern is not an institutional spokesperson. Similarity of audience does not erase real differences.
- Mark all produced knowledge files `trust: low` until Alex reviews them.
