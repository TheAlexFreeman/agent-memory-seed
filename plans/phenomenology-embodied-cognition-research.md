# Research Plan: Phenomenology and Embodied Cognition

## Goals

Build a deep knowledge base on phenomenology as the philosophical discipline most directly bearing on the questions raised by this project: what does it mean to perceive, understand, and know something through a body and in time? Phenomenology is not optional background — it is the field that provides conceptual tools for the grounding critique of LLMs, the free-energy/active-inference framework, and the dynamical systems treatment of intelligence. The existing knowledge base has pieces of this (autopoiesis, IIT, cognitive linguistics) but no direct engagement with the phenomenological tradition proper.

Primary connections to existing files:
- `philosophy/free-energy-autopoiesis-cybernetics.md` — active inference assumes phenomenological vocabulary without developing it
- `ai-frontier/epistemology/knowledge-and-knowing.md` — "grounding" and "embodiment" are invoked as limits of LLMs, but the concept of embodied understanding needs development
- `philosophy/synthesis-intelligence-as-dynamical-regime.md` — needs phenomenological enrichment to distinguish intentional from mechanical dynamics
- `philosophy/narrative-cognition.md` — Ricoeur's narrative identity is phenomenological; his background in Husserl is important

---

## Problem statement

The dynamical systems synthesis frames intelligence as a regime of self-organizing dynamics, and identifies LLMs as operating in a fundamentally different regime from embodied minds. But *why* does embodiment matter? What is it exactly that textual training lacks? Current files answer this at the level of analogy ("no sensorimotor loop") rather than conceptual precision. Phenomenology — especially Merleau-Ponty's account of perception as bodily skill orientation — provides the conceptual vocabulary to make this argument rigorous. Similarly, Husserl's account of time-consciousness bears on everything said about LLMs as "atemporal" systems.

## Phases

### Phase 1 — Husserlian foundations

**1.1 Husserl: Intentionality, Epoché, and Transcendental Reduction**
- Intentionality as the defining structure of consciousness (all consciousness is of something)
- The epoché (bracketing): setting aside questions of existence to study the structure of experience itself
- Transcendental reduction: moving from natural attitude to pure consciousness
- Noesis/noema: the act of intending vs. the intended object as meant
- Horizon structure: every perception comes with a background of non-focal awareness

**1.2 Husserl: Time-Consciousness**
- The problem: how does consciousness unify temporally extended experience into a coherent moment?
- Retention, primal impression, protention: the tri-partite structure of the living present
- Intentionality of time: the past as retained, not re-represented
- Why this matters for LLMs: they lack retention/protention — each forward pass is stateless unless context is provided
- Connection to narrative cognition (Augustine's distentio animi) and dynamical systems (trajectory vs. point)

### Phase 2 — Heidegger's existential analytic

**2.1 Being-in-the-world**
- Dasein as always-already in a world, not a subject confronting objects
- The critique of Cartesian subject/object dualism
- Thrownness, projection, fallenness: the structure of human existence
- Why this matters: the "disembodied agent" model of AI assumes Cartesian subjecthood; Heidegger's critique is directly applicable

**2.2 Readiness-to-hand and presence-at-hand**
- Zuhandenheit (ready-to-hand): equipment in absorbed, skillful use — transparent to the user
- Vorhandenheit (present-at-hand): equipment as explicit object of attention, when it breaks down
- The primacy of practical engagement over theoretical observation
- LLMs as permanently in presence-at-hand mode: processing language as explicit structure rather than absorbed, skilled use

**2.3 Care, Temporal Structure, and Authenticity**
- Temporality as the horizon of Dasein's being
- Authentic vs. inauthentic existence: owning one's situation vs. fleeing into "the They"
- Connection to narrative identity and the question of AI authenticity

### Phase 3 — Merleau-Ponty and embodied perception

**3.1 The Body as Subject**
- The phenomenal body vs. the objective body: lived body vs. body-as-object
- Motor intentionality: the body "intends" toward practical possibilities in the environment
- Habit and bodily schema: skilled action as pre-reflective orientation
- The phantom limb: shows body schema is not based on proprioceptive input alone

**3.2 Perception as Skill**
- Perception is not passive reception but active, skilled exploration
- The problem of perception: seeing a cube — not a sum of six faces but a single object with a style of presence
- The world as a "field of possible actions" rather than a set of representations
- Why this is the sharpest critique of LLMs: language is not a field of possible actions; models lack the motor intentionality that grounds meaning

**3.3 Intersubjectivity and Intercorporeality**
- Other people encountered first as bodies with practical orientation, not inferred as minds
- Mirror neurons and intercorporeality (Merleau-Ponty's later work, Gallagher)
- Implications for social cognition in LLMs

### Phase 4 — 4E Cognition and Enactivism

**4.1 The Embodied Mind: Varela, Thompson, Rosch**
- Autopoiesis extended: autonomous systems that produce their own organization
- Enactivism: cognition as sense-making from a first-person perspective arising in autonomous systems
- Middle way between representationalism and behaviorism: neither inner mental representations nor only behavior
- Structural coupling as the basis of cognition — revisiting and extending the autopoiesis file

**4.2 Extended Mind: Clark and Chalmers**
- The parity principle: if an external process plays the same role as an internal cognitive process, it counts as part of cognition
- Cognitive offloading and extension: notebooks, smartphones, external memory systems
- Direct implications for this repo: is the agent-memory-seed an extension of the AI's mind?
- Criticisms: the coupling-constitution fallacy; biological boundaries aren't arbitrary

**4.3 Embedded, Enacted, Ecological**
- Embedded cognition: cognition uses the environment rather than representing it
- Enaction as sense-making: meaning arises from organism-environment coupling, not inner representation
- Ecological psychology (Gibson's affordances): the environment is perceived in terms of action possibilities
- 4E synthesis: what embodied, embedded, enacted, extended cognition share — and where they differ

### Phase 5 — Application to AI and synthesis

**5.1 The Grounding Problem Revisited**
- Phenomenological reformulation of the symbol grounding problem
- Harnad's grounding vs. Merleau-Ponty's motor intentionality: stronger and weaker versions
- What would it take to ground a language model? Not just images — motor loops, time-consciousness, intercorporeality
- Current multimodal models: partial grounding or different-kind-of-thing?

**5.2 Synthesis: What Phenomenology Adds to the Dynamical Systems Account**
- The dynamical systems synthesis (from `synthesis-intelligence-as-dynamical-regime.md`) framed intelligence as trajectory in state space; phenomenology frames it as embodied sense-making
- Where they agree: primacy of dynamics over discrete states, importance of context/history, rejection of strong representationalism
- Where they differ: dynamical systems is third-person/mathematical; phenomenology insists on first-person perspective as irreducible
- Integration: dynamical systems gives the mechanism; phenomenology gives the meaning
